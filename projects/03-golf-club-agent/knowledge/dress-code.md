# Dress Code

The club maintains a dress code on the course, on the practice ground, and in the clubhouse. It is not intended to be onerous. Its purpose is that members and their guests feel the club is being looked after, and that visitors arriving for an away fixture find a club that takes itself reasonably seriously.

Members are responsible for the dress of their guests. The pro shop will quietly have a word where necessary; it is far easier for everyone if that is not required.

## On the course

- A collared shirt must be worn. Polo shirts are acceptable and are what most members wear.
- Tailored shorts or trousers. Shorts must be smart and of tailored cut.
- Golf shoes with soft spikes. Metal spikes are not permitted anywhere on the course or in the clubhouse.
- Socks must be worn. With shorts, socks should be either ankle length or knee length — the mid-calf sports sock is not appropriate.

**Denim is not permitted anywhere on club premises.** This includes the car park, the practice ground, the clubhouse and the course, and it applies to denim of any colour or cut. The committee has considered relaxing this on several occasions and has declined each time.

Also not permitted on the course:

- Football, rugby or cricket shirts
- Vests, or shirts without sleeves (for men)
- Tracksuit trousers
- Cargo shorts or shorts with external patch pockets
- Beachwear of any kind

## Wet weather

Waterproofs of any reasonable design are permitted. Members caught out by the weather should use their judgement; nobody has ever been asked to leave the course for wearing an unsuitable waterproof.

## In the clubhouse

The dress code in the clubhouse is the same as on the course, with the exception that golf shoes should be removed or changed in the locker room before entering the lounge or the dining room.

Hats and caps must be removed indoors.

*(The spike bar operates its own standard — see the bar and clubhouse guidance.)*

## Practice ground

Full course dress code applies. The practice ground is visible from the road and is often the first thing a visitor sees.

## Juniors

Junior members are expected to observe the same standard. The club would rather a junior played in an inexpensive collared shirt than not at all, and no junior has ever been turned away on cost grounds — speak to the pro shop.

## Enforcement

The pro shop may decline to allow a player onto the course. In practice this is very rare and is usually resolved by lending something from the shop.

A member who disagrees with a decision should take it up with the Pro Shop Manager rather than with the staff member concerned.
