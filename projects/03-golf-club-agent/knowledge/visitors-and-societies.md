# Visitors and Societies

## Unaccompanied visitors

The course is open to unaccompanied visitors on weekdays outside competition periods. Visitors must book through the pro shop and pay the visitor green fee, which is set separately from the member guest fee and reviewed annually.

Visitors are asked to arrive at the pro shop at least twenty minutes before their tee time to check in and pay. A visitor who has not checked in ten minutes before their time may lose the slot to a member on the waiting list.

Weekend visitor play is not available except as the guest of a member, or as part of a booked society day.

Visitors must observe the dress code in full. The pro shop will explain it at check-in; most difficulties arise with denim, which visitors frequently assume is acceptable because it is at their own club.

## Handicap requirements

Unaccompanied visitors are asked to hold a handicap of 28 or better for men and 36 or better for women, or to be able to demonstrate a reasonable standard of play. This is a pace-of-play measure rather than a standard of exclusivity, and the pro shop applies it with common sense.

Visitors without a handicap may play accompanied by a member.

## Societies

Society bookings are taken for parties of twelve or more, Monday to Thursday, and occasionally on a Friday by arrangement. Society days are booked through the pro shop and require a deposit.

A society package normally includes:

- Reserved tee times over a two-hour window
- Use of the practice ground on arrival
- Bacon rolls and coffee before play
- A two-course meal in the dining room afterwards
- Prize table arrangements if requested

Societies are asked to nominate an organiser who is the single point of contact for the club, and who is responsible for the party's observance of the dress code and pace of play.

The club does not accept society bookings during July and August, when member and competition demand is highest, nor in the week before the Club Championship.

## Corporate days

Corporate days are handled in the same way as societies but with exclusive use of the first tee for the booked window, and the option of full course closure by separate arrangement with the committee. Course closure requires eight weeks' notice and is granted rarely.

## Away fixtures and reciprocal clubs

The club holds reciprocal arrangements with a number of clubs. Members wishing to play at a reciprocal club should ask the Secretary for a letter of introduction; arriving without one is a common source of embarrassment.

Visiting members from reciprocal clubs are welcome on weekdays at a reduced green fee. They should book through the pro shop and bring evidence of membership and handicap.

## Buggies and trolleys for visitors

Buggies are available to visitors subject to availability and are not bookable in advance. Electric trolleys may be hired from the pro shop.

Visitors bringing their own powered trolleys should note that the club requires them to be fitted with a functioning brake.

## Conduct

Visitors are guests of the club. The pro shop may decline a booking, or ask a visitor to leave, where conduct or pace of play affects members. This is very rarely necessary and is always a decision for the Pro Shop Manager rather than shop staff.
