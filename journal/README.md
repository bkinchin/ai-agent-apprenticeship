# Journal

Your learning record. **This is the most important directory in the repository.**

The code demonstrates that you built something. The journal demonstrates that you understood it — and it is what makes days 1–21 compound rather than blur.

## The rule

**A day is not complete until its journal entry is written.** Not when the code runs. When the entry exists.

## Files

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `TEMPLATE.md` | Copy this each day |
| `day-01.md` … `day-21.md` | Daily entries |
| `self-assessment.md` | Scores on days 7, 14, 21 |

## How to write these

**Write badly and honestly rather than well and defensively.** The value is in recording what confused you, what you got wrong, and what surprised you. An entry that says "went well, everything worked" is worth nothing to the person reading it in three months — who is you.

Specifically:

- **Record numbers.** Pass rates, token counts, costs, latencies. "It got better" is not a finding.
- **Record what you got wrong.** Especially where your first instinct was wrong, and why.
- **Record what you decided not to do.** Rejected options are half of an architecture.
- **Answer the reflection questions properly.** They are chosen to be the ones you would skip.

## Self-assessment

Score yourself against [SUCCESS_CRITERIA.md](../SUCCESS_CRITERIA.md) on days 7, 14, and 21:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | I have heard of it |
| 2 | I can define it |
| 3 | I can explain it with an example |
| 4 | I can explain the tradeoffs and defend a choice |
| 5 | I can design an alternative and say when it would be better |

Target on day 21: **4 or above on every item.** Anything at 3 or below names your next two weeks of work.
